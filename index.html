Part 1 • Warm-Up Foundations

What Are Forces? ⚙️

In science, a force is simply a push or a pull acting upon an object. Forces can cause an object to start moving, speed up, slow down, stop, or change direction.

Push vs. Pull

Push: Applying force to move an object away from something (e.g., closing a locker door, kicking a soccer ball).

Pull: Applying force to bring an object closer to something (e.g., pulling a rope, opening a drawer).

Applied vs. Non-Contact Forces

Applied Force: A contact force where a person or another object physically pushes or pulls an object.

Non-Contact Force: A force that acts over a distance without physical touching (e.g., gravity pulling down an apple, a magnet attracting steel).

Part 2 • Core Concept

Forces Have Magnitude & Direction 🏹

Forces are not just numbers—they are vector quantities! That means every force in the universe has two key features:

1. Direction (Where?)

Which way the force is pushing or pulling. This can be Up, Down, Left, Right, Forward, or Backward.

Example: Gravity pulls objects in the downward direction towards Earth's center.

2. Magnitude (How Strong?)

The strength or size of the force, measured in Newtons (N).

Example: Gently nudging a tennis ball has a small magnitude (2 N), while launching a rocket has an enormous magnitude (34,000,000 N).

How Scientists Draw Forces (Force Arrows)

Scientists use arrows to represent forces visually:

  • The arrow points: In the direction the force is acting.
  • The arrow length: Shows the magnitude (longer arrow = stronger force).
